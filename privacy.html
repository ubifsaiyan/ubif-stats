uBiF Web Extensions — Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 27, 2026

uBiF Web Extensions is a set of tools for uBreakiFix store staff. This policy explains what the extension does with information.

What stays on your computer

The extension reads the uBreakiFix NextGen Portal, eBay and Amazon pages you open so it can improve them (for example, showing part names or warning about a listing). It also reads the company's Special Order Parts Order Google form, only after you allow it and only in tabs it opened itself (see below). It saves your settings (including the portal color you picked), your eBay seller blacklist and cached part names in your browser's own storage. Your settings may sync between your own computers through your Chrome or Firefox account. None of this is sent to the developer or anyone else.

Usage stats

To show how much time the tools save, the extension counts how often they are used and sends those counts to the developer's Google Firebase database (project "uBiF Web Extensions Analytics"). Each record contains only:

  • which tool and what happened (for example "Autocomplete Service: reached Checkout", "Auto-Confirm Serial: confirmed", "IMEI Autofill: filled", "GD Exception: form submitted", "Pullbacks 2026: 12 parts marked for RMA, 0 failed"), and for Autocomplete Service the type of service (for example "Verizon In-Person Device Refresh"), how long the run took, where it started, and a short code for why it stopped; to find out why runs stop, also which step it was on, how long the IMEI step took, how long the tab was in the background, and, only when a run stops, the names of any boxes the portal marked as missing, the portal's own error messages and the title of any pop-up on screen (numbers, email addresses and the customer's name are taken out first); for the Verizon free accessory reminder, what was missing, which button was pressed and the $15 reimbursement it may have saved;
  • the uBreakiFix store number you're signed into in the portal;
  • a random ID for this browser profile and a random ID shared by browsers signed into the same Chrome or Firefox account (not linked to your name or email);
  • the extension and tool versions, the browser name and version, and whether it's a test copy;
  • the date and time it happened.

Once a day each copy also sends a check-in with the same IDs, store number and versions, plus which tools are switched on and which are switched off, each with its version number. No names, email addresses, customer information, work order contents or web addresses are sent. The records can only be read by the developer's stats page, signed in with an allowed Google account, and are used only to measure usage and time saved and to find problems. They are never sold or shared for any other purpose.

On Firefox nothing is sent unless you allow "technical and interaction data" (Firefox asks when you install, the toolbar menu asks once, and Settings → Usage stats can change it any time).

What is sent, and only when you choose to

If you use the Feedback button and press Send, the extension sends what you typed (the type of feedback, tool, subject, message, and the name and email address if you entered them), together with the extension version, your browser and operating system, and which tools are switched on. It is delivered by email to the developer (ubifsaiyan@gmail.com) through the Web3Forms service (web3forms.com). It is used only to fix problems and consider feature requests, and is never sold or shared for any other purpose.

If you choose "Open in Outlook" or "Open in Gmail" instead, the message is sent from your own email account, and that service's privacy policy applies.

If you press File GD Exception on a work order, the extension reads that work order (GSPN ticket number, IMEI, device model) and your portal login (your name and email, the store number and store email) and types them into Samsung's exception request form on forms-prod.sprinklr.com — the same form you would otherwise fill in by hand. Once every field is filled in, the extension presses Submit for you (you can switch this off in Settings and press it yourself), and when Samsung's confirmation appears it adds that confirmation text (with Samsung's ticket number) to the work order's notes in the portal. That information goes to Samsung only, when you press the button, and is kept in your browser's storage only for the moment it takes to open the form. Create GSPN Ticket asks the portal to create a GSPN ticket for the work order, exactly as the portal's own link does; nothing leaves the portal.

If you press Order Parts on a work order and then Submit, the extension opens the company's Special Order Parts Order Google form (docs.google.com) once per part and fills in what you chose in the pop-up: the store, district manager, type of repair, your answers to the form's questions, the work order number, the part link and anything typed under "SKU if known". It then presses Next and Submit on that form for you. That information goes to the company's purchasing team through Google Forms, exactly as if you had filled in the form by hand, and only when you press Submit. Afterwards the extension adds a note with the part links to the work order in the portal. The list of parts is kept in your browser's storage for up to a day so the result can be shown, and is cleared when the browser restarts. Nothing about it is sent to the developer.

Print Buyback Label (in a part's View details pop-up) reads that part's work order from the portal and sends the label to the DYMO printer on your computer. Nothing leaves the portal and your computer.

Pullbacks 2026 (managers only) looks up the serial numbers you scan or paste, in the portal. When you press Mark, it marks those parts for RMA in the portal with the reason "Pullback" and reason details that include your portal email (for example "2026 Q4 Pullbacks - Actioned by you@ubreakifix.com"), the same as doing it by hand. The list is kept in your browser's storage so it isn't lost if the page reloads. Nothing about it leaves the portal and your computer, except the usage count described above (how many parts were marked and how many failed; no serial numbers or part names).
